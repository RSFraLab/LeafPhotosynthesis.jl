Equilibrium and gas-exchange sensitivities ​

These calculations hold leaf temperature, absorbed radiation, atmospheric CO₂/O₂, surface humidity, and transport parameters fixed. Their scalar, immutable interface runs on CPU and CUDA; the core has no device or solver-library dependency. The runnable core example assembles parameters, an equilibrium solve, and the carbon/water derivatives.

Coupled equilibrium ​

Write net biochemical demand as A(Cc), in μmol CO₂ m⁻² leaf s⁻¹. Let P be pressure, u the explicit mol-to-μmol conversion, r the stomatal H₂O/CO₂ conductance ratio, gbc and gm the CO₂ boundary and mesophyll conductances, and gs stomatal H₂O conductance. Then

Cs = Ca - A(Cc) P / (u gbc)
gs = g0 + gain max(A(Cc), 0) / Cs
R  = 1/gbc + r/gs + 1/gm
F(Cc) = Ca - Cc - A(Cc) P R / u = 0
Ci = Cc + A(Cc) P / (u gm)

gain is assembled once from the chosen Ball–Berry/Medlyn law and prescribed surface humidity (Ball–Berry) or VPD (Medlyn); each law checks only the driver it reads. The ratio r of the Medlyn gain and of the stomatal resistance r/gs is the one diffusivity_ratio of the SeriesDiffusion. Although commonly called A–Ci iteration, this solver uses Cc as its scalar unknown, preserving finite mesophyll resistance. A lower bound of zero and upper bound Ca + max(-A(0),0) P R(g0)/u include respiratory release above ambient CO₂. Trial states that exceed boundary supply give a negative bracket residual without evaluating a singular target at nonpositive Cs.

SafeguardedNewton uses the analytic demand derivative and the target's dependence on A and Cs. Newton proposals must lie inside the current bracket. Explicit periodic bisection guarantees bracket contraction; Bisection supplies a comparison method. Thermal responses and electron supply are computed once per solve. Darkness/zero capacity has an analytic fast path. A caller may pass a warm start initial_co2 in Pa (the seventh argument, after excitation) from the previous time step; it broadcasts like the other arguments, so each leaf of a batch can start from its own previous Cc. No state or cache is hidden inside the model.

Both a pressure residual and flux residual must satisfy the configured absolute and relative tolerances. EquilibriumSolution holds the gas exchange at the solution in exchange (the fields of a gas_exchange result) and reports stomatal_conductance, status, iterations, bracket_width and the CO₂ residual. Iteration/precision failures return the best physical iterate; invalid inputs/brackets return NaN diagnostics. Always check is_valid(solution) before using fluxes. Tolerances are never silently loosened. The example's numerical settings are explicit in its TOML, separately for FP32/FP64. The bracket is intentionally conservative and may remain wider than the root error after rapid Newton convergence; the width is not a claimed uncertainty estimate.

A zero minimum conductance g0 is allowed (Medlyn fits commonly use it). A closed leaf needs CO₂ storage only when it respires on balance: in darkness, or in light below the light compensation point, no steady state exists and the solver returns EquilibriumInvalidBracket. In light the model's CO₂ compensation point Γ (respiration included) exists, and the result is the limit g0 → 0⁺: the open-stomata root in (Γ, Ca) when Ca − Γ > (P/u) r Ca / gain (EquilibriumConverged), otherwise the closed-stomata steady state with zero conductance, zero net assimilation and Cc = Γ (EquilibriumStomataClosed, a valid solution). With g0 = 0 the closed state is a steady state whenever Γ exists, so it can coexist with the open root; the solver returns the open root because it continues the unique solution for g0 > 0 as g0 → 0⁺, so the result does not jump when a fitted g0 approaches zero. Humidity and energy balance are prescribed here, so this is not a complete Bonan 12.1 reproduction. Prognostic stomata use the same target family with independent opening and closing timescales; tests show their long-time approach to the coupled equilibrium under constant forcing. See Liu et al. (2024) for the physiological distinction between instantaneous and dynamic conductance.

Analytic derivatives for optimization ​

Let G = u / (P R) be the total CO₂ conductance expressed in μmol m⁻² s⁻¹ Pa⁻¹, so A(Cc) = G (Ca - Cc). For independently varied gs, implicit differentiation gives

dG/dgs   = (1/R)^2 r u / (P gs^2)
dCc/dgs  = (dG/dgs) (Ca - Cc) / (∂A/∂Cc + G)
dA/dgs   = (∂A/∂Cc) (dCc/dgs)

carbon_assimilation_derivative evaluates ∂A/∂Cc analytically for minimum and quadratic co-limitation. assimilation_sensitivity returns dco2_dgs and dnet_assimilation_dgs; at fixed temperature respiration is independent of gs, so the latter is also the derivative of pre-respiration assimilation. Pre-respiration assimilation still includes photorespiration: it is not raw carboxylation. Canopy GPP requires an explicit convention and leaf-area aggregation outside this kernel.

Without an existing exchange solution, this function calls the analytical minimum-limitation/signed-diffusion closure. To use another supported equilibrium configuration, pass its valid solution (or its exchange) as the sixth argument (after excitation) and its stomatal_conductance as state; an exchange that is not a root for the passed excitation and state gives NaN. It differentiates steady CO₂ supply–demand at independently varied gs, not the empirical stomatal target law. The optimization law should decide how to use that gradient.

For fixed leaf-to-air vapor-pressure difference D and water boundary conductance gbw, WaterVaporDiffusion and transpiration_sensitivity provide

gw = 1 / (1/gs + 1/gbw)
E = gw D / P
dE/dgs = (gw/gs)^2 D/P

E is mol H₂O m⁻² leaf s⁻¹; negative D represents condensation. D is leaf-to-air, not the leaf-surface VPD used by the stomatal target. gbw is an independent explicit input, not assumed to equal gbc or inferred using the stomatal diffusivity ratio. No saturation-vapor-pressure formula is silently selected. dA/dgs has units μmol CO₂ per mol H₂O, while dE/dgs is mol H₂O per mol H₂O; cost functions must use compatible units. For example, an objective A − λE has slope A_g − λE_g.

At a minimum-limitation tie the derivative uses the Rubisco-capacity branch; at the clipped compensation threshold it uses the clipped side. These are one-sided choices, not smooth derivatives. Optimizers must account for such kinks.

Temperature feedback and optimality models ​

With a steady leaf energy residual B(T,gs)=0 and nonzero B_T,

dT/dgs = -B_g / B_T
dA/dgs (total) = A_g + A_T dT/dgs
dE/dgs (total) = E_g + E_T dT/dgs

Here B is reduced after any other quasi-steady variables have been eliminated; its partial derivatives must include those dependencies. A jointly coupled system can instead differentiate its small residual Jacobian. A prognostic energy model has a temperature tendency and does not automatically use this equilibrium derivative. The present package has no energy-balance component, so it does not report a temperature sensitivity. Returning zero would confuse prescribed temperature with a physical prediction of no feedback.

The inspected Emerald revision includes Anderegg, Eller, Sperry/Sperry2, and Wang/Wang2 optimality families. Their carbon–water/hydraulic costs require more than an empirical linear target coefficient; some express a gradient-driven tendency directly. Keep their cost/constraint laws and time evolution separate from the numerical root method. They have not been ported here. The current derivatives supply reusable building blocks without importing a whole hydraulic/SPAC system.

Validation ​

Tests compare full equilibrium against independently formulated BigFloat bisection, and sensitivities against high-precision centered differences away from kinks. Cases include dark/light, low and zero ambient CO₂, high VPD, weak/absent boundary resistance, both empirical target laws, both compensation policies, and minimum/smooth co-limitation. Failure statuses, warm starts, FT inference, and zero solver allocation are checked. GPU checks compare both precisions with CPU, including reordered populations and invalid input. These are equation and execution checks, not a fit to measurements or a complete upstream model validation.
