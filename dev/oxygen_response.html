Oxygen and the photorespiratory compensation point ​

The short example changes biochemical O₂ while solving C3 gas exchange with prescribed temperature and humidity. The interactive gallery compares an explicit oxygen-dependent Γ* component with a prescribed-Γ* control. Select temperature and Float32/Float64; all curves come from Julia, not browser-side equations.

The physical component ​

Γ* is the CO₂ partial pressure where carboxylation balances photorespiratory CO₂ release. For photorespiratory release λ per oxygenation and partial-pressure-based Rubisco specificity S, Γ* = λ O/S. At fixed temperature and unchanged specificity and stoichiometry, this gives Γ_(T,O) = Γ_(T,Oref) O/Oref. Farquhar, von Caemmerer & Berry (1980), §4.2, Eq.38. The supplied reference temperature law must be calibrated at the explicit Oref in Pa. There is no inferred ambient value or additional total-pressure factor. A concentration-based specificity would require the corresponding solubility conversion before using this pressure-based equation.

At Cc = Γ_, pre-respiration assimilation is zero and net assimilation is −Rd. Gross carboxylation, photorespiratory turnover and linear electron flow can remain positive. Γ_ is therefore different from the net CO₂ compensation point when respiration is nonzero. The oxygen budget uses realized turnover at the same Cc.

PrescribedCompensationPoint(response) explicitly retains a temperature-only Γ* law. OxygenScaledCompensationPoint(response, reference_oxygen) scales that law at the biochemical O₂ level. Both implement AbstractCompensationPoint and live in the existing C3TemperatureResponse.gamma_star slot. They do not change the C3Photosynthesis layout or the signed/clipped assimilation policy, which remains a separate component named assimilation_sign.

Call biochemical_rates(responses, T, O) when oxygen affects Γ*. All production environment-aware calculations use this interface: prescribed-CO₂ photosynthesis, turnover, diffusion, equilibrium and fixed-O₂ sensitivities. The two-argument form biochemical_rates(responses, T) remains valid for prescribed temperature responses, including custom user extensions. It deliberately has no oxygen-dependent fallback. Disabling oxygen diagnostics with NoOxygenExchange does not disable environmental O₂ in carbon biochemistry or stomatal responses.

Calibration and limits ​

This example keeps the existing Bonan-derived thermal calibration and declares its reference O₂ partial pressure explicitly in oxygen_response.toml. The fixed-Γ* curve away from that pressure is a controlled inconsistency demonstration, not a second calibrated physiological prediction. It illustrates why changing Rubisco competition alone does not represent a consistent oxygen perturbation.

Thermal calibration is replaceable. A recent sunflower study reports that the method used to fit Γ*(T) can materially change the inferred temperature response; it does not establish one new default for all vegetation. Moreno-Echeverry et al. (2026). No empirical coefficients from that study are silently substituted here.

The scalar scaled law returns zero at O=0, but full gas exchange with this treatment is validated for positive O₂ and Γ*. The present bounded equilibrium solver requires Γ*>0; degenerate zero-O₂/zero-CO₂ limits and O₂ limitation of respiration are separate work. No epsilon floor, internal O₂ diffusion, anaerobic metabolism, acclimation or evolving atmospheric O₂ is implied.

Validation and reproduction ​

Independent BigFloat temperature/specificity checks, reference-O₂ equivalence, oxygenation/carboxylation and electron/O₂ stoichiometry, compensation-point turnover, analytical diffusion residuals, equilibrium, fixed-O₂ conductance derivatives, NoOp parity, type inference and zero warmed scalar allocations are tested in both precisions. CPU/CUDA batches reorder dark and lit cases and retain native solver diagnostics. The unchanged Bonan exercise remains covered by its existing oracle. When the component was added (November 2026), the full suite passed 35,348 CPU checks and 5,594 CUDA checks, and two independent reviewers cleared the scientific implementation and example. The oxygen charts of the gallery are generated and checked by the documentation build. The validation record pins sources, software, tests and the separate code/biophysics reviews.

sh
julia --project=. examples/oxygen_response.jl
julia --project=. -e 'using Pkg; Pkg.test(test_args = ["oxygen"])'
julia --project=test/gpu test/gpu/runtests.jl
julia --project=docs docs/make.jl

Main examples call the biophysics. Setup, TOML loading and table writing remain in examples/setup/; no I/O, grid or device dependency enters the core package.
