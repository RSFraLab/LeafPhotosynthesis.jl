On this page

Design decisions ​

This package supplies leaf photosynthetic biochemistry for both classroom exercises and larger models. Its initial scope is C3 assimilation at prescribed leaf temperature, absorbed light, and biochemical-site gas partial pressures. These choices are provisional until tested against more models and coupled applications.

Physical and software boundaries ​

LeafModel contains concrete photosynthesis, stomatal, and oxygen components under abstract interfaces. Explicit no-op types disable optional calculations. C3Photosynthesis composes temperature responses, electron transport, stoichiometry, rate limitation, and compensation treatment. Composition determines the compiled calculation through Julia dispatch. Model parameters are immutable; inputs and outputs are scalar values, not mutable per-leaf scratch containers. Every kind of component has an abstract type (temperature laws, compensation points, co-limitation rules, assimilation-sign treatments, excitations, stomatal targets and models, diffusion, oxygen exchange, fluorescence, quenching, solver methods), and the composite constructors require it, so a value of the wrong kind is a MethodError at construction. The C3 and Cytochrome models share their leaf entry points through AbstractC3Photosynthesis; the methods through which those entry points reach a model are internal, and a new electron-supply model is added inside the package.

The audited PSII fluorescence source is composed alongside LeafModel, sharing its actual PSII excitation and realized linear electron flow. Its rate-competition and quenching interfaces do not constrain future coupled photosystem families to the same inputs. Spectral optics remain external. The excitation interface is a separate scalar input, so pigment selection and spectral optics do not enlarge LeafModel or its gas/temperature environments. BroadbandExcitation preserves existing components; PrescribedPSIIExcitation{FT} carries already allocated same-PAR PSII photons. New excitation families opt in through validity, electron supply and PSII diagnostic methods. Unsupported component/input combinations fail dispatch instead of silently reverting to broadband light.

Dynamic induction/NPQ would have explicit state owned and advanced by the caller; no hidden relaxation on each function invocation.

Stomatal and mesophyll diffusion are distinct from biochemical demand. The core gas-exchange layer analytically closes diffusion at known conductance for signed C3 with minimum limitation and reports its residual. The prognostic stomatal component exposes a tendency. A separate safeguarded scalar solver closes C3 assimilation, CO₂ diffusion, and Ball–Berry/Medlyn targets at prescribed leaf temperature and surface humidity. Numerical methods are types independent of stomatal physics. Analytic sensitivities hold temperature and transport parameters fixed; energy-balance feedback must be supplied explicitly before interpreting total optimization gradients. Leaf energy balance and hydraulics can be separate components without placing soils, canopy geometry, or atmosphere solvers in this package.

Canopy optics, radiative transfer, air transport, spatial discretization, forcing, parameter-file parsing, plotting, and integration orchestration stay outside the core. Use existing solvers where suitable. Decide which components deserve separate packages when working interfaces and independent reuse justify them, not from a diagram alone.

Units and parameters ​

Core units are K, Pa for both gas species and kinetic constants, and μmol m⁻² leaf s⁻¹ for molecular/photon/electron fluxes with the species stated explicitly. Net carbon uptake is positive. A pre-respiration assimilation rate already includes photorespiration and is not raw carboxylation. Leaf area to ground area conversion belongs to canopy aggregation. Absorbed light is not incident light; absorption belongs to optics.

Every scientific value is provided explicitly, including physical constants, reference temperature, empirical curvature, quantum yields, and stoichiometry. Example TOML files contain units and provenance. No plant species or experimental parameterization is silently selected by the core. The source may contain mathematical identities and powers used by equations. Constructors use FT; parsing converts numbers once on the host. Hot calculations must not force Float64 or parse configuration.

Biochemical-site CO₂ is prescribed. Using intercellular CO₂ assumes negligible mesophyll resistance. The Bonan exercise fixes Ci/Ca externally; this is not a stomatal closure. Its mole-fraction kinetic constants and gases are converted together at an explicit reference pressure. This conversion is not a validated altitude response. Legacy gamma-star temperature responses remain prescribed. Explicit PrescribedCompensationPoint and OxygenScaledCompensationPoint components select the convention in the existing gamma_star slot. The latter requires a declared reference O₂ partial pressure and all environment-aware paths pass O₂ through biochemical_rates(responses,T,O). It adds no ambient default or empirical recalibration. See oxygen response for the positive-O₂ gas-exchange domain, specificity convention and zero-O₂ limitations.

Numerical and hardware contract ​

Same-FT inputs and parameters must give the same FT in every numerical diagnostic. The number type of a result is the promotion of the values it holds, so a dual number in any parameter (a co-limitation curvature, a stoichiometry) or forcing value carries through. The core must infer concrete return types, allocate no per-leaf heap storage after compilation, and avoid host indexing, printing, exceptions, or file I/O in hot methods. Mathematical input domains are documented; caller-side validation precedes large batch launches. Invalid physics must not be silently replaced by plausible default values.

Precision rule. Every continuous scalar reachable from the arguments of an entry point or component function (the model and its components, environment, state, drivers, diffusion, excitation) must have one binary precision; dual numbers count with the precision of their values, so ForwardDiff.Dual{T,Float64} mixes with Float64. A mismatch is a MethodError of the called function, raised at its entry by require_compatible (the warm start of the equilibrium solver is such an argument: a value of another precision, or an integer, is a MethodError). The check reads only types and is folded away when the method is compiled, so it costs nothing in a kernel; no precision mismatch is turned into NaN or promoted silently. Composite constructors (C3Photosynthesis, CytochromePhotosynthesis, the temperature bundles, LeafModel, and every struct with components) apply the same rule on the host, so a Float32 model cannot hold a Float64 component. The converting form T{FT}(…) converts floats; an integer, Bool or rational in a continuous field is a MethodError in every constructor form.

Domains and branches on primal values. Every domain check and every branch at a kink reads primal values (is_positive, is_nonnegative, is_fraction, exceeds, is_at_most, primal_min, primal_max, primal_clamp), so whether an input is valid never depends on a derivative seed (ForwardDiff ≥ 1.0 orders dual numbers with equal values by their tangents). At a tie a kink returns the one-sided derivative ForwardDiff 0.10 would: MinimumLimitation the first capacity (Rubisco), NonnegativeAssimilation zero at Cc = Γ*, a stomatal target its minimum conductance at A = 0. The exception is the exact-darkness test of the analytic diffusion root, which also looks at the tangent so that a dual light of value zero propagates its derivative; it does not change validity.

One result shape, one validity predicate. The entry points return NamedTuples whose fields extend each other: gas_exchange returns the fields of photosynthesize (carbon fluxes, electron_transport and the model's electron diagnostics) followed by the diffusion fields, and an EquilibriumSolution holds such a gas-exchange result in exchange beside the solver diagnostics. Invalid input never throws; it gives NaN in every field of a NamedTuple result, or a status (EquilibriumStatus, FluorescenceStatus) in a result struct. is_valid(result) tells a usable result from an invalid one for every result type.

What may throw. Nothing callable inside a kernel throws for a value. Structs split in two classes:

  • Per-leaf, per-time-step quantities, which a canopy model builds inside a broadcast, never throw for a value; invalid values lead to NaN results (or an invalid status) through the validity predicates of the entry points: LeafEnvironment, GasExchangeEnvironment, StomatalState, StomatalDrivers, the excitations, SeriesDiffusion and WaterVaporDiffusion (conductances vary with wind and leaf size), the rate bundles C3Rates and CytochromeRates, the input bundles OxygenFluxInputs, PSIIFluxInputs and CoupledPhotosystemInputs, and the result structs.

  • Fixed parameters, built once on the host, may reject invalid values in their constructor with an ArgumentError: temperature laws and bundles, electron-transport and Cytochrome parameters, stoichiometries, limitations, compensation-point laws, stomatal targets and models, oxygen exchange, solver settings, fluorescence models and quenching laws, rate parameters of the photosystems, state-transition rules, PAM observers, and the composite models. Validation that needs forcing (rates at a temperature, ATP balance at an O₂) stays in the entry points' predicates. BallBerry and Medlyn stay in this class: a target is set once per leaf type on the host, so an invalid slope or minimum conductance is reported where it is entered instead of as NaN stomatal outputs many steps later. (A per-leaf target built inside a kernel works as long as its values are valid.)

Scalar kernels should work in ClimaCore broadcasts and standalone GPU kernels. Device selection and array layouts belong to adapters/callers. CUDA and Metal support are acceptance targets, not claims established by using parametric types. Require real backend tests, including reordered dark/lit populations and cross-device numerical comparisons. FP32 and FP64 are CPU/CUDA targets; Apple GPU work should target FP32. Continuous scalar fields and environments accept ForwardDiff.Dual tangents alongside Float32 and Float64, under the precision rule above, and reject discrete integer or rational types; model kinks at clipping and limitation transitions are explicit and tested against 256-bit BigFloat reference derivatives.

Only a leaf that respires on balance needs CO₂ storage to be described; that is outside this quasi-steady core.

Stomatal drivers and the diffusivity ratio ​

Each stomatal law checks only the humidity driver it reads: Ball–Berry the leaf-surface relative humidity (in ), Medlyn the VPD (finite). The other driver may be NaN, so a model that supplies only the driver its law uses gets a valid result; an invalid driver the law reads gives NaN, never the minimum conductance. The same predicate (valid_humidity) serves the target, the tendency, leaf_tendencies and the equilibrium solver. The H₂O/CO₂ ratio of stomatal conductances (1.6) is stored once, as SeriesDiffusion.diffusivity_ratio: the diffusion path converts the stomatal H₂O conductance to CO₂ with it, and the Medlyn law reads it from the same diffusion (target_conductance(target, drivers, diffusion)), so the two can no longer disagree. The boundary-layer conductances for CO₂ (SeriesDiffusion.boundary_co2) and water vapour (WaterVaporDiffusion.boundary_water) are separate inputs; no ratio between them is stored.

Teaching contract ​

Students call the production component functions, inspect named diagnostics, then compose a complete leaf calculation. No parallel teaching implementation of the same equations. Each example identifies its source exercise, assumptions, parameter file, units, expected curves, and validation status. Main scripts focus on biophysics; TOML parsing, parameter assembly, and output formatting live in named setup files. Documentation plots are generated from the same example functions during the documentation build and shown as interactive Chart.js charts. Plotting dependencies remain outside the Julia core; notebooks are optional. Include only exercises solvable within this package's leaf-physics scope. Bonan 11.1 is implemented; 12.1 is a candidate for discrete leaf gas-exchange components at prescribed temperature. Separate implemented cases from future work. Combined exercises will live in a separate example repository later, composing the individual physics packages without broadening this one into a land or canopy model.

Biochemical turnover and fluorescence inputs ​

biochemical_turnover exposes gross carbon turnover and realized linear electron flow for the signed C3 closure, independently of the optional oxygen component. Available light-driven electron supply and realized biochemical use remain distinct. The same positive capacity drives carbon and oxygen calculations, avoiding a 0/0 carbon-efficiency reconstruction at compensation. See the equations and limits. This API does not select an NPQ or fluorescence-yield parameterization; the separate fluorescence component does. Leaf optical escape, canopy transport, and atmospheric transfer belong to separate components with explicit photon, spectral, and area conventions.

Atmosphere coupling ​

Leaf physiology reports local source responses to local temperature, CO₂, humidity when needed, and absorbed radiation. It does not own canopy air. Keep radiation layers, leaf angle/light populations, and the physical air grid independent, with conservative mappings. Future coupling must assign storage once, exchange equal/opposite fluxes, and close ground–leaf–air budgets. Mean fluxes alone may not supply the atmospheric turbulence boundary: momentum drag, turbulent energy, scalar statistics, and nonlocal transport need explicit scientific review, particularly within the roughness sublayer.

Within-layer illumination handed to leaf physiology ​

Canopy radiation must supply a leaf-area-weighted APAR distribution within each layer, not only its mean. The canopy package owns continuous leaf orientations, within-layer depth integration, illumination/exposure assumptions, and quadrature. LeafPhotosynthesis evaluates each representative population with its own absorbed photon flux and physiological state; the caller integrates the resulting fluxes.

For a layer of positive one-sided leaf area index ΔL, representative leaf-area fractions w_i and absorbed PAR q_i [μmol photons m⁻² leaf s⁻¹] must satisfy sum(w_i)=1 and ΔLsum(w_iq_i)=Q_layer_leaf, where Q_layer_leaf is independently resolved leaf-only PAR absorption in μmol photons m⁻² ground s⁻¹. Keep energy and photon budgets separate, using explicit spectral conversion. Empty layers return no leaf populations, without division by zero. Pigment-specific absorption is a separate diagnostic with its convention stated explicitly.

Continuous orientation PDFs do not eliminate numerical quadrature. Illumination can be a mixture of sunlit/shaded populations and need not have a smooth APAR PDF. The number of representative populations is a numerical choice, converged against both absorption and integrated physiology, rather than a fixed biological leaf count. A two-stream solver needs an explicit angular closure to reconstruct leaf exposure; its hemispheric fluxes alone do not uniquely determine the APAR distribution. Multistream radiance resolves more angular information but does not, by itself, resolve spatial gap variability or the temporal statistics of sunflecks.

Individual leaves and their exact sunfleck trajectories are not tracked. For prognostic stomata and future induction/NPQ, represent physiological memory statistically: a joint distribution of illumination and physiological state, or a weighted ensemble of representative histories with an explicit exposure-transition model. These are statistical representatives, not identified physical leaves. A marginal APAR distribution alone does not specify temporal exposure. Independent redraws each timestep impose a timestep-dependent decorrelation assumption; use explicit persistence/duration parameters and test timestep convergence instead.

Keep temporal exposure separate from stationary RT. A small sun/shade Markov closure is a possible starting experiment, not a validated default: it needs transition timescales and its stationary weights must match the assumed illumination statistics. Changing forcing also needs a consistent transient treatment. More general duration distributions may be required by observations. For finite sampled ensembles, maintain the radiation budget with an explicitly conservative weighting scheme; ensemble-mean conservation is not identical to per-step conservation.

Begin with steady physiology integrated over the APAR distribution. Evaluate dynamic closures separately using light sequences with equal APAR histograms but different persistence. Where light fluctuations are much faster than a physiological state, investigate averaging that state's tendency over the light distribution, rather than evaluating it at mean APAR. For slower or comparable fluctuations, illumination–state correlations need to be retained. No temporal closure is yet selected or implemented; representative histories must not be presented as resolved canopy geometry.
