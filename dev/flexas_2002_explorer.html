Flexas et al. (2002) explorer ​

Why only coupled Cytochrome b₆f mechanics reproduce drought fluorescence: an interactive comparison of six steady-state chlorophyll fluorescence and SIF yield formulations in LeafPhotosynthesis.jl, overlaid on the subpixel-digitized light-response trajectories of Flexas et al. (2002, Physiologia Plantarum 114:231–240, Figs. 2, 3C & 5) across six water-stress stages in Vitis vinifera cv. Tempranillo.

Every curve, residual, peak and RMSE value is computed in Julia when the documentation is built; switching precision loads an independent Float64 or Float32 run. Digitized Flexas et al. (2002) points are strictly observational overlays: no parameter was fitted to them. The Johnson & Berry (2021) rate constants are used unchanged, and the detector weighting εF1/εF2 = 2.0 is carried over from Johnson & Berry (2021), who fitted 2.0 (interquartile range 1.6–2.5) for a LI-6800 on Populus fremontii; it is not a calibration of the Walz PAM-2000 that Flexas et al. used. The following inputs were chosen by the author and have no Tempranillo-specific source in this repository: the non-stomatal Hill schedule (half-conductance 56 mmol H₂O m⁻² s⁻¹, exponent 4, floors 0.60 for Vcmax and 0.85 for Cyt b₆f), Vqmax = 174 μmol e⁻ m⁻² s⁻¹, gm = 0.2 mol CO₂ m⁻² s⁻¹ and Γ* = 3.70 Pa. The schedule matters: with stomatal diffusion alone the lit Figure 2 RMSE is 0.097 and the Inset RMSE 0.144; with the schedule they are 0.062 and 0.038.

Single-variable empirical KN(x) models lock every curve to an identical peak ceiling, whereas two-state Cytochrome b₆f with the PAM observer tracks peak suppression, a leftward peak shift, and high-light sub-Fo quenching simultaneously. Three equations explain every curve above ​

1. Single-variable \(K_N(x)\) peak invariance

Empirical rate-competition models parameterize regulated heat dissipation solely as a function of relative PSII light saturation \(x = 1 - \Phi_P / \Phi_{P0} \in [0, 1]\):

\[ \frac{\Phi_F(x)}{\Phi_{F,o}} = \frac{1 - \Phi_{P0}(1 - x)}{(1 - \Phi_{P0})\left[1 + \dfrac{K_N(x)}{K_F + K_D}\right]} \]

Lowering \(g_{sw}\) or \(V_{c\max}\) rescales where \(x(\mathrm{PPFD})\) lies along the irradiance axis, but every sweep traverses the exact same scalar curve, locking global peak height at \(1.462\) (\(x^* \approx 0.19\), Cotton) or \(1.453\) (\(x^* \approx 0.23\), Drought) at every conductance.

2. Two-state Cyt \(b_6f\) + PAM-2000 baseline

Johnson & Berry (2021) decouple open PSII trap fraction \(q_2(Q_1, V_{q\max})\) from stromal sink yield \(\Phi_{P2}(Q_2, C_c, V_{c\max})\), observed through long-pass (\(>700\;\mathrm{nm}\)) PSI/PSII weights \(\varepsilon_{F1}/\varepsilon_{F2} = 2.0\), carried over from their fit for a LI-6800 on Populus fremontii:

\[ \frac{F_s}{F_o} = \frac{\varepsilon_{F2}\,\alpha_2\,\Phi_{F2} + \varepsilon_{F1}\,\alpha_1\,\Phi_{F1}} {\varepsilon_{F2}\,\alpha_{2,\mathrm{d}}\,\Phi_{F2,o} + \varepsilon_{F1}\,\alpha_{1,\mathrm{d}}\,\Phi_{F1,o}} \]

Redox-invariant PSI emission (\(\sim 39\%\) of dark \(F_o\)) yields dark apparent \(F_v / F_m = \mathbf{0.821}\) (measured: \(0.80\text{–}0.83\); the agreement depends on carrying the weighting over to the PAM-2000), while earlier \(A_j \to A_c\) crossover under drought truncates the \(q_2\)-driven rise at progressively lower \(F_s / F_o\).

3. Photorespiratory electron-sink floor

In ambient air (\(O = 21\;\mathrm{kPa}\)), even at \(C_c = \Gamma_*\) where net carboxylation and photorespiration cancel in carbon uptake, gross oxygenation and cyclic PSI flow (\(\eta(\Gamma_*) = 13/12 \approx 1.083\)) keep consuming electrons:

\[ J_{P700,c}(\Gamma_*) = \frac{13\,\Gamma_*\,V_{c\max}}{\Gamma_* + K_c(1 + O/K_o)} \]

At \(25\;^\circ\mathrm{C}\), \(J_{P700,c}(\Gamma_*) \approx 0.799\,V_{c\max}\): for \(V_{c\max} = 96\), pure stomatal closure cannot push \(J_{P700,c}\) below \(76.7\;\mu\mathrm{mol\;e^-\;m^{-2}\;s^{-1}}\) (\(\approx 79.3\) as \(g_{sw} \to 0\) with \(R_d = 0.80\)), which is why the model needs a non-stomatal downregulation (an author-chosen schedule) to follow the severe-drought curves (\(g_{sw} < 60\)).
