05 / SUPPLY AND USE

Electron flow survives carbon cancellation

At the photorespiratory compensation point Γ*, carboxylation balances photorespiratory CO₂ release. Both processes still turn over, so realized linear electron flow remains positive even though pre-respiration carbon uptake is zero. Net uptake additionally subtracts respiration.

Read the complete turnover example

Light and temperature are fixed; CO₂ is prescribed at the biochemical site. The example uses signed C3 assimilation and computes turnover with oxygen exchange disabled. Alternative electron sinks, cyclic flow and fluorescence yield are not inferred. Equations and limits →

07 / MECHANISTIC VS. EMPIRICAL STRESS FLUORESCENCE

How fluorescence responds when sinks or Cytochrome b₆f limit

Compare empirical PSII quenching fits (van der Tol et al. 2014 Cotton and Drought KN(x)) against the coupled Cytochrome b₆f + PSII/PSI mechanics of Johnson & Berry (2021), using shared Rubisco kinetics (Vcmax25 = 60) and aligned mid-PAR crossover capacities (FvCB Jmax25 = 100.2, θ = 0.90 vs. Cytochrome Vqmax25 = 195, SerialLimitation).

julia --project=. examples/cytochrome_stress.jl
Read the complete Cytochrome stress-comparison example

Under high-light stomatal closure, empirical Cotton KN(x) saturates early and predicts a slight fluorescence yield increase, requiring a separately fitted Drought parameter table. Johnson & Berry's single mechanistic parameter set predicts strong feedback quenching under Rubisco/stomatal sink stress, PSI cyclic-flow rebalancing at moderate light, and opposite fluorescence signs for Rubisco vs. Cytochrome b₆f downregulation. Yields remain internal leaf emission probabilities before optical escape. Biophysics, equations and stress regimes →

08 / JOHNSON & BERRY (2021) BENCHMARKS

Connectivity, state transitions, capacity inversion, and PAM leakage

Reproduce four signature mechanisms from Johnson & Berry (2021, Photosynthesis Research 148:101–136, Table 2 & Fig. 8): PSII exciton sharing (KU2, Case 9), closed-form pointwise LHCII state transitions (StateTransitionRule, Case 10), coordinated Cytochrome b₆f + Rubisco capacity scaling (Case 12), and apparent leaf-chamber PAM fluorometry under PSI detector leakage (PAMFluorescenceObserver, Eqs. 38–42).

julia --project=. examples/johnson_berry_2021.jl
Read the complete Johnson & Berry (2021) benchmark example

With static antennae (Case 9), exciton sharing (KU2 > 0) boosts PSII efficiency above PSI's capacity and forces low-light KN > 0, damping the huge puddle (KU2 = 0) fluorescence spike. Closed-form state transitions (Case 10) instead migrate 17–22% of PSII cross-section to PSI, keeping KN = 0 up to Rubisco saturation and restoring a moderate biphasic ΦF2 peak while boosting light-limited assimilation. Coordinated capacity scaling (Case 12) shifts that peak in PAR and flips the sign of ∂ΦF2/∂capacity between low and high light. In long-pass (>700 nm) PAM detectors (εF1/εF2 = 2), PSI contributes ~39% of dark Fo, depressing apparent 1 − Fs/Fm′ and lake 1 − qL while leaving puddle qP invariant when KX1 = KP1. Closed-form derivations and PAM equations →

09 / FLEXAS ET AL. (2002) WATER-STRESS BENCHMARK

Steady-state Fs / Fo across drought and SIF yield formulations

Test how every chlorophyll fluorescence formulation in LeafPhotosynthesis.jl reproduces—or structurally fails to reproduce—the grapevine water-stress experiment of Flexas et al. (2002, Physiologia Plantarum 114:231–240, Figs. 2, 3C & 5): six drought stomatal conductances (g = 150, 80, 62, 55, 39, 10 mmol H₂O m⁻² s⁻¹), the PPFD = 750 Fs / Fo vs. g inset, high-CO₂ / O₂-depleted reversion, and DTT xanthophyll inhibition.

julia --project=. examples/flexas_2002.jl
Read the complete Flexas et al. (2002) water-stress benchmark

Single-variable empirical KN(x) fits (van der Tol et al. 2014 Cotton and Drought) obey an invariant peak-height constraint (max Fs/Fo ≈ 1.45–1.46 across all g), and Cotton KN(x) inverts the drought sign at high light. Coupling CytochromeFluorescence with Walz PAM-2000 long-pass PSI sensitivity (PAMFluorescenceObserver, εF1/εF2 = 2, giving dark Fv/Fm = 0.821 vs. measured 0.80–0.83) reproduces the progressive decline in peak Fs / Fo, leftward shift in peak irradiance, high-light sub-Fo quenching (Fs / Fo < 1), curvilinear Fs / Fo vs. g inset, and Fig. 3C high-CO₂ reversion.
